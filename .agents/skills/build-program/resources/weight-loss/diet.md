# Weight Loss: Diet and Habits

Principles below align with Greg Doucette’s *Circle Diet*, his cookbooks, and his general coaching philosophy on sustainable fat loss. They are **food for thought or places to start**, not dogmatic rules to follow to the letter.

---

## Fundamentals

### Calories in vs. calories out

Energy balance is the constraint that matters for weight change: if you eat fewer calories than you burn, you lose weight. Day-to-day noise does not override average intake and expenditure over time.

### Track bodyweight over time, not day to day

Use trends, not single weigh-ins. A practical approach is to look at **median weight over roughly three weeks** to see whether you are trending up, down, or flat. One day can be an outlier (salt, sleep, stress, GI contents).

### Average calorie balance matters

A deficit on one day does not “fix” a surplus on other days. What matters is **average** intake vs. needs across **days, weeks, and months**. Weekly swings can hide whether you are actually in a net deficit.

---

## The diet approach

### High-volume, low–calorie-density foods

Prefer meals that fill you up for fewer calories—large portions of lower-calorie-density foods—instead of tiny portions of very calorie-dense meals. The goal is to reduce hunger and the urge to binge on hyper-palatable junk.

### Prioritize protein

Include a **protein source at each meal** to improve satiety and support muscle retention while dieting. Research has shown 1g protein per lean pound of body mass is the sweet spot.

### Calories first; don’t obsess over macros

Focus on **total calories per meal** and across the day. Perfect macro splits are less important than controlling total energy intake and hitting protein in a sustainable way.

### Dieting should not mean suffering

Build a plan you can **enjoy and repeat**. Food choices you like make adherence easier than white-knuckling through misery.

---

## Mindset and habits

### Lifestyle, not a temporary diet

Lasting results come from **sustainable habits** you can maintain—not a short crash phase you abandon afterward.

### Avoid extremes

Aggressive crash diets and reckless bulking both tend to backfire. **Small, consistent** adjustments usually outperform dramatic swings.

### Good enough beats perfect

Nutrition labels and database entries can be wrong. **Weigh food when it helps**, but don’t spiral over precision. Aim for consistency and directionally correct tracking rather than perfection.
