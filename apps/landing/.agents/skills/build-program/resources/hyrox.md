# Hyrox

Apply these guidelines when designing or editing a **Hyrox** program: indoor fitness racing that alternates **running** with **fixed functional stations** in a standard order worldwide. The goal is **fastest total time**, not a powerlifting total or a physique show.

These notes are **principles and reference points**, not a substitute for the official Hyrox rulebook, venue briefings, or medical advice. **Always confirm** the athlete’s **division**, **season**, and **event-specific rules** before competition-specific loading and targets.

---

## What Hyrox is

- **Format:** Athletes complete **eight** segments of **~1 km running** and **eight workout stations** in a **fixed order** (run → station → run → station…). Total running distance is often cited around **~8 km on the course plus ~0.7 km in the Roxzone** (venue-dependent).
- **Scoring:** **Elapsed time** from start to finish (after the last station, athletes typically sprint to the finish arch-confirm current race flow for the event).
- **Training goal:** Build **running durability** at race pace, **strength-endurance** and **technique** on each station, **grip and core** under fatigue, and the ability to **repeat efforts** after running-not to maximize a single lift or muscle isolation.

---

## Race flow (station order)

After each **~1 km run**, the athlete performs the next station in this **fixed** order:

| # | Station | Prescription | Primary demand |
|---|---------|--------------|----------------|
| 1 | **SkiErg** | 1000 m | Upper-body pull endurance, posture, pacing |
| 2 | **Sled push** | 50 m (often 4× ~12.5 m) | Leg drive, core, power-endurance |
| 3 | **Sled pull** | 50 m (often 4× ~12.5 m) | Posterior chain, grip, pulling endurance |
| 4 | **Burpee broad jumps** | 80 m total | Full-body power, hip extension, pacing |
| 5 | **Row** | 1000 m | Full-body endurance, leg drive, rhythm |
| 6 | **Farmers carry** | 200 m | Grip, traps/core, walking strength-endurance |
| 7 | **Sandbag lunges** | 100 m | Single-leg strength, core, sandbag handling |
| 8 | **Wall balls** | 100 reps | Squat endurance, shoulder stamina, accuracy |

**Erg stations (SkiErg, row):** distance is fixed; effort and pacing vary by athlete.

## Training principles

1. **Specificity (run + work):** Hyrox rewards **pacing the run** and **executing stations efficiently** while tired. Early prep can separate running and gym work; closer to the event, add **combined** sessions (easy or race-pace runs plus station pieces, or multi-station circuits) so the athlete adapts to **transitions** and **grip fatigue**.
2. **Aerobic base:** Sustained easy-to-moderate running builds **durability** and helps **1 km repeats** feel repeatable. Include **threshold / tempo** and **interval** work at or near **target race pace** for key runs.
3. **Strength-endurance over max strength:** Prioritize **submaximal, repeatable** efforts (higher rep squats, lunges, carries, wall-ball sets, rowing/SkiErg intervals) over pure 1RM-style work unless the athlete needs a strength foundation first.
4. **Grip and core:** Sled pull, farmers, and late-race wall balls punish a weak grip or sloppy midline. Train **carries**, **pulling**, and **anti-extension / bracing** in a fatigued state when appropriate.
5. **Weak-link training:** Identify **slow or costly** stations (and slow run segments) from time trials or experience; bias **extra technique and volume** there without abandoning the full race picture.
6. **Periodization (flexible):** Common prep lengths are often **~10-14+ weeks**, moving from **general capacity** (base running + strength) toward **more Hyrox-like** combinations and **simulations**, then a **taper** (reduced volume, maintained sharpness). Adjust to the athlete’s level, injury history, and days per week.

## Weekly structure (examples only)

Individualize by **sessions/week**, **experience**, and **equipment**. Patterns that appear often in Hyrox-oriented coaching:

- **Run + strength:** One day **longer easy run**; separate days for **heavy-ish strength** (squat/hinge/lunge patterns, carries) and **conditioning** (row/SkiErg intervals).
- **Quality running:** One session with **1 km repeats** or **threshold** work at goal race effort.
- **Bricks:** **Short run** → **station block** (e.g. sled + farmers, or row + wall-ball volume), building duration and specificity over time.
- **Simulation:** Partial or full **race-style** order (e.g. multiple rounds of **1 km + 1-2 stations**) in the last block-**not** every week early on.

## Exercise and equipment mapping (`getExerciseList`)

- **Rows / pulls:** Support **SkiErg** and **row** demands (lat endurance, posture); use machine and cable rows, pull-ups, deadlifts/RDLs as available.
- **Legs:** Squats, split squats, lunges, leg press for **wall balls** and **sandbag**; step-ups or lunges for **lunging** under load.
- **Loaded carries:** Farmers handles, dumbbells, kettlebells for **farmers carry** patterns.
- **Power and conditioning:** Burpees, broad jumps (if available), erg intervals; **sled** push/pull when equipment exists.
- **Running** may not exist as a template exercise: put prescribed **distance, pace, or intent** in **`routine notes`** and/or the exercise **`reason`** (and tie it to the day’s goal in the questionnaire).

If the gym **lacks** SkiErg or sled, use **closest available stimuli** (e.g. row intervals for aerobic pulling; heavy pushes on turf or leg drive on bike **with caveats**) and state the **limitation** in **`reason`** / **`notes`**-do not invent exercise IDs.

## RPE and pacing

- **Strength and station work:** Use **RPE** and **technical quality**; avoid **daily failure** on high-skill or high-systemic-fatigue work during heavy running weeks.
- **Key run intervals:** Prescribe **effort or pace bands** (e.g. “at or slightly below target Hyrox run pace”) for **specific** sessions; keep **easy runs** truly easy to absorb volume.
- **Early mesocycles:** Limit **full race simulations** so the athlete doesn’t accumulate **chronic fatigue** before the **specific** block.

---

## When applying

- **Primary frame:** Hyrox = **run + eight stations in order**; programming should **reflect** that mix, not a **pure powerlifting** or **pure bodybuilding** split unless the user explicitly asks for that as secondary work.
- **`buildProgram`:** Use **session titles** that reflect intent (e.g. “Run + posterior chain”, “Threshold + row intervals”, “Hyrox brick - farmers & wall balls”). Put **run prescriptions** in **`notes`** / **`reason`** when not represented as an exercise.
- **`reason`:** Name **which station or limiter** the work supports (e.g. weak sled pull, grip, wall-ball pacing) and **how many days/week** they train-same standard as other program types.
- **Optional cross-load:** If the user also wants **fat loss** or **muscle balance**, you may load [`weight-loss/`](./weight-loss/) or [`bodybuilding.md`](./bodybuilding.md) **secondarily**-Hyrox **specificity** still drives exercise choice and weekly layout.
- Use **only** exercise template IDs returned by **getExerciseList**; never invent IDs.
